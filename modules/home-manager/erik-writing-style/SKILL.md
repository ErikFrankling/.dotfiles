# How Erik writes

These rules come from Erik's own corrections. They are mainly from the DA2210 HW4 session (7 Oct 2026). On 21 Oct 2026 HW1/HW2 drafts came out too polished and he asked for a full rewrite. They apply to **all** text he writes, not one course.

## Rules

1. **Write like a normal person, like a student.** Use short plain sentences and everyday words. "I think" and contractions (don't, can't) are fine.
   - No literary or polished phrasing: no "so-called", "of course", "that is the asymmetry X describes", "it is worth noting".
   - If a sentence sounds like an AI or a textbook, rewrite it the way a student would say it out loud.
2. **Use only the references the question needs.** Every reference is something Erik has to verify, so cut decorative ones.
   - Example: HW1 went from 9 references to 8 (Tarski out), and HW2 from 7 to 5 (Aristotle and Popper out).
   - Remove unused entries from the .bib file too.
3. **No subjective big claims.** Don't call things "the biggest", "the most important" or "one of the leading", and don't write "most researchers believe" without a source.
   - Use neutral wording ("well established", "still open") or leave the claim out.
   - Opinions are fine when they are clearly his ("I think…").
4. **Use the exact terms from the source.** Use the paper's own keywords and names (e.g. the Ariane 5 report's "horizontal bias"), not invented ones.
   - Paraphrase faithfully: simplify, never change the meaning.
5. **Never write about process.** Don't say how or where something was read or found ("I read the free arXiv version", "I found it on Semantic Scholar") unless the assignment asks for it.
6. **Be precise.** No vague sentences where the reader can't tell what "it" refers to. Each sentence says one clear thing.
7. **Structure like his finished work.** For school LaTeX use unnumbered lettered headings, `\subsection*{1) ...}` and `\paragraph{a) ...}`, 12pt, 1.5 spacing and roughly 2 pages.

## Process

- Before drafting, read his earlier finished text in the same course, or HW4 above.
- After drafting, check every sentence against rules 1–6.
- Tell him which facts and references he should verify himself.
- Never send or submit anything for him. He sends it himself.
- For emails: short, friendly and casual, not long or formal. He has said a draft was "way too long, be a bit nicer and chill".
